## 01. Read this first: the seven rules

- **One plan, one tracker.** The plan is the Playbook ([playbook.html](playbook.md)). Log every problem and topic in your revision tracker. If it is not logged, it did not happen.
- **Depth over count.** Seniors' advice converges on about 250–300 well-chosen problems with patterns understood, not 1,000 rushed ones.^([\[1\]](#-sources)[\[2\]](#-sources)) Your plan already covers this through Striver A2Z plus the 123 worked solutions in this kit.
- **Stuck rule.** Think for 25 minutes (15 for easy, 40 for hard). Then read the solution, close it, write it yourself, and schedule redos at 1, 3, 7 and 21 days.
- **Be ready before you ask.** A referral only gets you the interview; you still have to pass it.^([\[3\]](#-sources)) Ask for referrals once you can solve most mediums in 30 minutes.
- **Apply early.** Internship applications often open 6–8 months before the start date.^([\[4\]](#-sources)) Section 4 lists the windows.
- **Proof beats claims.** Two deployed projects, a GitHub with tests and CI, and write-ups of your security labs. Your resume should point to things a recruiter can open.
- **Weekly review, every Sunday.** 30 minutes: what slipped, what to cut, next week's three priorities ([weekly-planner.pdf](weekly-planner.md)).

This plan is short on purpose. Each line points to the file in the kit that has the detail. Dates for outside programmes come from the sources listed at the end; always confirm on the official page before applying.

### Every week

- **Saturday:** one OA set under exam conditions (90 minutes), from the date the set lists. Score it the same day.
- **Sunday:** the week's 30-minute self-test ([weekly-revision-tests.pdf](weekly-revision-tests.md)), then weekly review (30 min), redo the week's failed problems, plan next week.
- **From Feb:** one Codeforces contest a week. **From Mar:** one mock interview a week; two a week from 31 May.
- **Rest:** one half-day off a week. Burnout costs more days than it saves (a senior's warning in your own plan).

### The loop, every week from February

- Keep a sheet: company, role, link, date applied, referral asked (who), status, next action.
- Ten tailored applications and five referral messages a week. Tailor the top three resume bullets to the job's keywords.
- Follow up once after 7 days. Never send the same message twice to one person.
- After every OA or interview, write down every question within an hour and add the hard ones to Anki.

## 09. The security and cloud track (your edge)

- **Your story:** an SDE who understands security and cloud, with TenantWall as proof. Every interview gets the two-minute TenantWall pitch ([grilling script](mock-interview-scripts-2.md)).
- **Labs, in order:** Bandit (Linux), TryHackMe beginner and SOC paths, PortSwigger SQL injection and XSS, then Hack The Box. Write up each lab in your notebook; publish clean versions on GitHub.^([\[15\]](#-sources)) ([owasp-portswigger-lab-map.pdf](owasp-portswigger-lab-map.md))
- **A home lab for SOC roles:** collect logs from your own virtual machines and practise spotting failed logins, new services and odd processes; list it on the resume.^([\[18\]](#-sources))
- **Certificates:** AWS SAA-C03 is in the plan. Security+ or the Google Cybersecurity Certificate are optional extras; do not take them before placements unless a target role asks.^([\[13\]](#-sources))
- **Ethics:** only test systems you own or platforms that invite it. One unauthorised scan can end a security career before it starts.

## 11. Mistakes seniors warn about

- **Too many resources.** One sheet, one tracker. Switching sheets resets your progress.
- **Watching instead of solving.** A video counts only after you code the problem yourself the next day.
- **No timed practice.** Untimed practice does not prepare you for a 90-minute OA.
- **Asking for referrals too early.** Get ready first; you get one first impression per person.^([\[3\]](#-sources))
- **Fancy resumes.** Graphics and columns break ATS parsing; exaggerated numbers break interviews.^([\[19\]](#-sources))
- **Applying late.** Many roles fill early; start in February at the latest.
- **Skipping Core CS.** Many technical rounds include OS, DBMS and networks questions; one hour a day is enough.
- **Projects with no proof.** No deployment, no tests, no README means the project is invisible.
- **Comparing yourself on LinkedIn.** Compare with your own tracker from last month.
