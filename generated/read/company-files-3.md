## Oracle (software engineer, campus)

Hires many freshers through campus drives in Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Noida. Reports describe a fundamentals-heavy process at a fresher level.

### What candidates report

- Online assessment: in one Bengaluru report, 2 coding questions plus MCQs on CS fundamentals; a Noida report also mentions MCQs with DSA.
- Interviews: a 2025 Hyderabad report describes two technical interviews and an HR round after the online test; others say 3 to 4 rounds in total.
- Reported topics: graphs, linked lists, DP, DFS, multithreading, and in-depth questions on your projects; one report adds a little system design.

### How your plan prepares you

- Core CS MCQ bank and one-pagers for the online test
- OA sets for the 2 coding questions
- Explain one project end to end, including what you would change

### Common mistakes

- Ignoring the MCQ section
- Not knowing basic multithreading (threads, locks, race conditions)

### Questions to expect

- A graph or linked-list problem
- Process vs thread; how a mutex works
- Walk me through your project

## Cisco (software engineer, fresher)

Networking company with large teams in Bengaluru; a good fit for a cyber-security CSE student because networking questions appear alongside DSA.

### What candidates report

- Online assessment: reports mention coding questions together with networking and computer-fundamentals MCQs.
- Interviews: commonly two technical rounds, sometimes a manager round, then HR; some drives merge technical and managerial rounds.
- Reported topics: DSA, OOP, OS, DBMS and computer networks (TCP/IP, subnetting, routing).

### How your plan prepares you

- Computer Networks one-pager and the CN section of the MCQ bank
- OA sets for coding
- Security lab notebook for networking commands and concepts

### Common mistakes

- Weak networking basics despite the company's domain
- Thin OOP answers

### Questions to expect

- Explain the TCP three-way handshake
- Subnet a /26 network
- A medium DSA problem

## Swiggy (SDE-1)

Food and quick-commerce platform in Bengaluru. Reports describe DSA plus a machine-coding or LLD round with high expectations.

### What candidates report

- Early rounds: an online assessment or a DSA round (two problems in one 2025 report).
- Machine coding or LLD: reports describe building a small system in a fixed time (45 to 90 minutes) or a demanding LLD discussion; for Android and frontend roles the build is app or UI work.
- Later rounds: hiring manager or HR, and for some candidates system design.

### How your plan prepares you

- LLD pack: rebuild designs in 60–90 minutes
- Backend starter for clean layering and tests
- Mock 6 and the LLD mock script

### Common mistakes

- Working code with no structure
- No discussion of extensibility or edge cases

### Questions to expect

- Design and code a small system (for example a library or ordering service)
- Two DSA problems
- Why this class design?

## Zomato / Eternal (SDE-1)

Zomato's parent is now called Eternal (Gurugram). Reports for 2025 freshers describe a selective process: DSA rounds and a culture-fit round.

### What candidates report

- Usually two technical rounds with one or two DSA problems each, then a managerial or culture-fit round; some reports add an online assessment first.
- Reported technical content: DSA (for example minimum swaps to sort), designing an LRU cache, resume deep dives, CS fundamentals.
- The culture round focuses on motivation, mindset and why Zomato.

### How your plan prepares you

- DSA mocks and OA sets
- LRU cache from the LLD pack and Worked Solutions
- Story bank: prepare 'why this company' with specifics

### Common mistakes

- A generic answer to 'why Zomato?'
- Not knowing the LRU cache cold

### Questions to expect

- Minimum swaps to sort an array
- Design an LRU cache
- Why do you want to join us?

## CRED (backend engineer / SDE-1)

Fintech in Bengaluru known for a long machine-coding round. Fresher entry is often through internships; reports for fresher full-time roles are few.

### What candidates report

- Machine coding: a timed in-memory build, reported at 2 to 2.5 hours, judged on clean state handling, structure and tests.
- Reported tasks include a payment-instrument recommender, metrics over a timestamp range, and an in-memory contact directory.
- Other rounds: DSA and backend fundamentals (databases, APIs, concurrency such as optimistic vs pessimistic locking).

### How your plan prepares you

- Backend starter: layering, validation and tests
- LLD pack under a 2-hour timer
- DBMS one-pager: transactions, isolation, locking

### Common mistakes

- No tests in the machine-coding submission
- Global mutable state instead of clear classes

### Questions to expect

- Build an in-memory service with clear interfaces and tests
- Optimistic vs pessimistic locking
- A sliding or rolling-window calculation

## CrowdStrike (security company, early career)

Endpoint-security company with teams in Pune and Bengaluru; a strong match for a security profile. Public reports for new-grad roles in India are scarce, so treat this page as a starting point.

### What candidates report

- An April 2025 India report for the sensor team describes an online round of 3 DSA problems, after which only the top few candidates moved to interviews.
- Earlier early-career reports mention a HackerRank screen, a recruiter call and a technical discussion.
- Most 2025 India reports are for experienced roles and describe a selective process.

### How your plan prepares you

- OA sets under strict timing (ranking cut-offs)
- C++ depth and OS one-pager: endpoint agents are systems software
- Security lab notebook and TenantWall for security conversations

### Common mistakes

- Treating a ranked OA like a pass/fail one: every solved problem counts
- No systems knowledge (processes, memory, syscalls)

### Questions to expect

- Three DSA problems in an online round
- How an operating system isolates processes
- Tell me about a security project
