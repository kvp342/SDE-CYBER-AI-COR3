## How to use the company files

*Level 2: Interview-ready*

One page per target company: how its process runs, what each round tests, and which parts of your plan prepare you for it. Use it in the week before an interview, together with the mock kit.

**Key idea**

The week before an interview: read the company page, then 3 to 5 interview posts from the last six months for your exact role (LeetCode Discuss, GeeksforGeeks, Glassdoor). Note the round types and any repeated questions.

Then run the matching mocks from the kit: DSA mocks for coding rounds, Mock 6 for machine coding or LLD, Mock 7 for design, Mock 5 for Core CS, Mock 8 for behavioural.

Map the company's values to your six STAR stories (Amazon Leadership Principles, Google's Googleyness, Atlassian's values).

Based on candidate reports from 2024 and 2025 (anonymous and self-reported). Processes change every year and differ between on-campus and off-campus: read 3 to 5 recent posts for your exact role before the interview.


**Points**

- Off-campus: apply within 48 hours of posting and ask for a referral
- Ask the recruiter for the round structure; they usually tell you
- After each interview, write down every question within an hour

**Traps**

- Preparing only from company-tagged lists without fundamentals
- Trusting one old post about the process

**Practise**

- [LeetCode Discuss: interview experiences](https://leetcode.com/discuss/interview-experience)
- [GfG interview experiences](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/interview-experiences/)

## Amazon (SDE intern and SDE-1)

*Level 2: Interview-ready*

The most common big-tech target for off-campus applicants from India. Two things decide it: DSA and the Leadership Principles.

**Key idea**

Online assessment: reports describe 2 coding problems of about medium difficulty in 70 to 90 minutes, followed by a Leadership Principles (work-style) assessment; some include a work simulation.

Interviews: about 2 to 3 rounds of around an hour, mixing DSA problems with behavioural questions. Each interviewer is reported to be assigned 2 or 3 Leadership Principles to test, so the whole loop covers them all.

Reported topics: arrays and hashing (Two Sum-style), trees, graphs, heaps, OOP and resume projects.

Based on candidate reports from 2024 and 2025 (anonymous and self-reported). Processes change every year and differ between on-campus and off-campus: read 3 to 5 recent posts for your exact role before the interview.


**Points**

- Your plan: Striver core plus the Hard pass covers the DSA; company-tagged sets in the sprint
- Prepare 6 STAR stories and map each to 2 or 3 Leadership Principles (Ownership, Dive Deep, Bias for Action, Learn and Be Curious, Deliver Results, Customer Obsession)
- Run DSA Mocks 1 to 4 and Mock 8 the week before

**Traps**

- Strong code but vague STAR answers
- Using 'we' instead of 'I' in stories
- Not stating complexity

**Interviewers ask**

- Two Sum and its variants
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with your team
- Tell me about a time you failed

## Google (STEP intern and university graduate)

*Level 3: Advanced*

The hardest DSA bar of the usual targets; system design is minimal for freshers.

**Key idea**

Reports describe a screening round, then about 3 to 4 coding and DSA interviews of 45 to 60 minutes, plus a Googleyness (behavioural) round; some loops add team matching.

Googleyness is described as comfort with ambiguity, humility, collaboration and doing the right thing.

STEP internships are for first- and second-year students; as a third-year you target the summer SWE intern and university-graduate roles.

Based on candidate reports from 2024 and 2025 (anonymous and self-reported). Processes change every year and differ between on-campus and off-campus: read 3 to 5 recent posts for your exact role before the interview.


**Points**

- Your plan: Striver plus the Hard pass, then contests for speed
- Practise clarifying questions: Google problems are often deliberately vague
- Run Mocks 3 and 4 twice; prepare Googleyness stories

**Traps**

- Jumping to code without clarifying
- No optimisation after the brute force

**Interviewers ask**

- A graph or DP problem with follow-ups that change constraints
- Tell me about a time you worked with unclear requirements

Later rounds: DSA (problem solving and data structures), a hiring-manager round, sometimes an LLD discussion.

Based on candidate reports from 2024 and 2025 (anonymous and self-reported). Processes change every year and differ between on-campus and off-campus: read 3 to 5 recent posts for your exact role before the interview.
